# ADR 0003: Playback, automatic stream choice and subtitles

- Status: accepted for Phase 2; checked on the webOS 22 TV on 27 October 2026 (results at the end)
- Date: 27 October 2026

## Context

Phase 2 makes BannerTV play. The plan asked for a stream picker, a remote-driven player, subtitles, audio switching, resume and plain error messages. ADR 0001 set the ground rules from the first TV runs on the LG 65NANO766QA (webOS 22, Chromium 87): the TV's own player lists MKV audio tracks but no embedded subtitles, native HLS hides alternate audio and subtitles, and with `mediaOption` the clock reads 0 at the first `playing` event. On 27 October 2026 Eyitayo asked for Watch to pick a working stream itself, the way Netflix's Play button does, instead of showing a list of links to people who do not know what they mean. The research behind this ADR (webOS media APIs, what addons send, hls.js and Shaka on the TV) was checked a second time against primary sources; its corrections are folded in below.

## Decision

1. **Three engines, the TV's own first.** The native `<video>` element plays files and plain HLS: it is the only path with full hardware decoding, HDR and Dolby audio. hls.js 1.7.3 takes HLS whose master playlist has a subtitle rendition, two audio renditions in one group, or audio in two languages (`needsHlsJs`, an RFC 8216 attribute parser). Shaka Player 5.3.2 (the DASH-only build) takes DASH. If the playlist cannot be read, native gets it. Before the first picture, a failure in one HLS engine hands the link to the other, which also covers hosts that block hls.js's own segment requests (CORS): native needs no CORS.
2. **Engines as separate scripts.** hls.js and Shaka are copied into `vendor/` at build time (`apps/webos/build/vendor-scripts.ts`) and loaded with a `<script>` tag the first time a stream needs them; the app imports their types only. A dynamic `import()` would have pulled Vite's preload helper, which needs `import.meta.url`, into the classic bundle. The app bundle stays under its 600 KB budget (156 KB gzipped); the two players have their own 450 KB budget (393 KB). Both pass the ES2017 syntax check; the ES2018 feature check skips them, since they guard their newer calls.
3. **Engine settings from the checks.**
   - hls.js: buffer limits in seconds (15, at most 30, and 10 behind), since hls.js never caps by bytes and shortens them itself when the TV's buffer fills. No MediaCapabilities filter on the TV, which misreports and would drop the 4K, HDR and 5.1 levels (Shaka turns it off on webOS too). Tracks are told apart by language and name, because hls.js renumbers ids within each group. TS E-AC-3, append and codec errors count as "format" at once; other media errors get at most two recoveries, five seconds apart. A worker that fails after starting is left to hls.js, which demuxes without it from then on and keeps the chosen audio and subtitles.
   - Shaka: only `bufferBehind` is lowered (10 s); its forward buffer is already small. Shaka 4 differs in text selection and preferences, so it is not a drop-in swap. Subtitle cues are walked recursively, since TTML nests.
   - Native: on the TV a resume uses `mediaOption` (`transmission.playTime.start`), and on a UHD TV HLS and DASH also lift `adaptiveStreaming` to 3840x2160 (its default caps at 1080p). Off the TV it seeks after `loadedmetadata`.
4. **Watch picks the stream.**
   - Every installed stream addon is asked in parallel, once focus has rested for 1.5 s on Watch, Resume or an episode on Details. Home never asks, and nothing is asked during playback. Answers are kept for five minutes, so the player usually starts without asking again.
   - Each stream is read for resolution, HDR type, video codec, audio format and channels, size, seeders, debrid service and cached state (`packages/core/src/streams/facts.ts`), then judged against the TV (`apps/webos/src/platform/capabilities.ts`): `tv.model.edidType` for DTS and TrueHD, the webOS version where that is missing, the system property `UHD`, the configs `tv.model.supportHDR` and `tv.config.supportDolbyHDRContents` for HDR10 and Dolby Vision, and Media Source checks for HEVC (at the 1080p level, so Full HD TVs that decode it pass) and AV1.
   - Torrents, links that need proxy headers (Chromium overwrites the TV's own request headers), YouTube and web links never auto-play. Streams that would likely give no picture or no sound (DTS or TrueHD the TV lacks, HEVC or AV1 without a decoder, Dolby Vision with no HDR10 base on a TV without it, 4K on an HD TV, uncached debrid links) go to the back.
   - "Best quality" ranks by the resolution the TV can show, cached state, HDR, release source, the addon's record on this TV, seeders and the addons' order; camcorder copies go last. "Fastest start" puts cached 1080p and smaller files first. Ties keep the addons' order with an explicit index, because Chromium 68 does not sort stably.
   - In both, a dub comes after every stream in the audio language wanted (the title's last choice, else the profile's): one that names only other languages, judged by the same rule as the Quality menu, where a stream that says nothing counts as the film's own language, since Torrentio flags only other languages. With nothing chosen, a stream that names only languages other than English is the dub. When the wanted language is not English, a stream that says nothing comes after those that name it. This comes straight after how likely a stream is to play, before the picture: a 4K Spanish dub no longer beats an English 1080p release (improvements list PL1). All streams and Sources sort the same way. When what plays has no track in the language (its tracks name others, or name none and the stream names only others), the Audio tab's last row offers "English audio: switch stream", in the language wanted or the app's own, to the stream closest to what plays that has it (`streamInLanguage`); it plays from the same place, and the language becomes the choice for the title.
   - Then the profile's picture limits (Settings > Languages and picture): with a highest picture set, streams known to be above it come after the rest, and with Avoid HDR on, HDR streams do too; a resolution not stated is never above the limit. The Quality menu still offers every resolution.
   - In both, a stream too heavy for the connection comes after every stream within it, and the further over the limit, the later (in steps of twice the limit), so when every stream is too heavy the lightest still comes first. Its average bitrate is its size over the title's running time (two hours for a film and one for an episode when the addon gives none), against a limit of 40 Mbit/s until the TV shows otherwise (item 7). 4K web releases and Blu-ray encodes sit under that; 4K disc remuxes, at 60 to 80 Mbit/s with higher peaks, sit over it. On the test TV an 84 GB remux picked first buffered again and again, and many LG TVs have 100 Mbit/s Ethernet and less over Wi-Fi.
   - The player waits until every addon has answered, or two seconds after the first playable stream arrives.
   - A title started with no stream or release given (Watch, Resume, Continue Watching) starts the release played last for it first, as the next episode does (item 13): the same addon's stream in the same binge group, else that addon's first playable stream from the same release group (improvements list PL2). `createReleaseMemory` keeps, per profile on this TV, the addon id, binge group and release group of the stream that last played 30 s of a title, at most 300 titles, never a link; a step-down that then plays replaces the stream that kept buffering. Not a broadcast's, a clip's, a sports event's or a Lab file's. When the addon no longer sends the release, the usual choice.
5. **One link test at a time.** On Details, after the rest, up to three top candidates are opened on one hidden, muted video element for their metadata only, with a two-second cap each, so a dead link is skipped before Watch is pressed. Moving focus or opening the player stops it and releases the decoder: most LG TVs have one. Settings has link testing on, off or automatic; automatic is off on webOS 5 until the floor TV is measured.
6. **Failing over.** A stream that errors, does not start within 30 s, or stalls for 20 s mid-play is marked failed (neither clock runs while the viewer has paused), and the next candidate starts at the same position with a short "trying another" note, at most three times. A stream picked by hand from All streams does not fail over. After 30 s of playback the addon counts as having worked.
   - A short clip of the video is a stream that failed: once its length is known, a stream under five minutes of a title the page says runs 20 minutes or more (`isMuchShorter`; never against an assumed running time, and never for a shorter title, whose running time can be far off for one episode) is a sample, a trailer or a debrid service's "not ready yet" clip (on the TV, a 30 s clip labelled 4K came first for a Breaking Bad episode and marked it watched). Auto-play moves on from it at the same place; with nothing left the problem box says it is only a short clip. One the viewer picked (Sources, All streams) plays, but nothing it plays is saved as the video's place, so it never marks the video watched; Up Next never counts over it, and at its end the problem box says it was only a short clip, with the ways out, instead of playing the next episode or leaving. Another stream, or the clip opened again (Back from All streams, Try again), starts where the clip was opened, not at the clip's clock.
   - A stream that keeps buffering is too heavy rather than broken: three pauses of a second or more, or 15 s of buffering, within two minutes. It is judged as soon as the pause going on would tip it over, so one long pause steps down at 15 s, before the 20 s stall counts the link as dead. Pauses within 10 s of a jump do not count, and neither does the loading a jump causes in a pause already going on. The bitrate is the lower of size over the running time and size over the video's length. The connection's limit is lowered, and the best candidate needing at least 30% less data (or a lower resolution when sizes are unknown) starts at the same position with a "switching to a lighter one" note, at most twice. A stream the viewer chose in the Quality menu or All streams stays, with a note that points to Quality when it offers something lower, or to Sources. The player shows one note at a time: switching, then trying another, then buffering, then subtitles.
7. **Which addons work, and what the connection keeps up with, remembered.** `createSourceMemory` keeps a halving tally of plays and failures per addon id, at most 100 addons, in IndexedDB, and ranks with a Laplace score. It never stores a stream link, which can carry a debrid key. `createBandwidthMemory` keeps one number, the bitrate limit: a stream that kept buffering lowers it to 75% of that stream's bitrate (never under 4 Mbit/s); ten minutes of playback in a row without buffering raises it to that stream's bitrate when higher, or lifts a lowered limit by a quarter (up to 40 Mbit/s) when the stream was at least half of it. A lowered limit is also lifted by a quarter for each full day since it was saved, so one bad evening does not stick. A stream that played smoothly and then kept buffering lowers the limit to where it was before that stream raised it, at most, and one that kept buffering says nothing good about the connection afterwards. Only the limit and when it changed are stored.
8. **Ways out.** The player's Quality menu offers the best stream at each resolution (4K, 1080p, 720p) and switches at the same position; the resolution already playing stays with the stream playing. Given what plays now, each resolution offers the stream closest to it (`qualityChoices` with a `QualityReference`): the audio language heard first, then light enough and cached, then the same release and family of source; samples, trailers and files far too small for the length playing are left out, and a choice that names only other languages says which. "Trailer", "teaser" and "featurette" count as a clip only after the title or as the whole name, so Trailer Park Boys is not one. "All streams" (from Details, the player and the problem screen) lists every stream with badges, "Tested: plays", and the reason a stream cannot play here, or its most serious note (no sound or picture first, then "a very large file", then a different look) with a count of the rest; it can be sorted by quality, fastest start or addon. A quick filter narrows All streams and Sources alike (picture size, cached only, addon, audio language; `filterStreams`), from a Filter button beside Sort that opens a menu of the four parts, each with how many streams have each value, and is kept for the profile (`streamFilter` in its preferences): a stream of unknown size passes no size filter, one that names no language or says MULTi counts as English (Torrentio flags only other languages), the stream playing stays listed in Sources, and a filter that leaves nothing, such as one kept from another title, shows every stream with a line saying so. When nothing plays, the problem screen says why in plain words and offers another stream, All streams, Try again and Back.
9. **Subtitles are drawn by BannerTV.** Every source ends as cues in one renderer: addon files (SRT, VTT, ASS/SSA as styled text), hls.js cues and Shaka cues, and on Android the lines its player hands over (ADR 0005, decision 6). Addons are asked with the stream's hash and size, and its file name, so they can match the release. OpenSubtitles v3 is not sent the file name: it answers byte for byte the same without it (measured on films and episodes, even with a name it lists itself), so every stream of a video shares its one answer and the name is not sent for nothing; other addons may use it. The hash is read from the one file a link names, with two byte ranges (and a plain request stopped at its headers when the range answer hides the file's size), and never from a playlist, a torrent, or a link whose addon asked for request headers: the page cannot send them, and the host is not asked without them. Of each file in an addon's answer the player keeps its name and what the addon says of it (`parseSubtitle`): the name is the addon's label, else its title, else the file's own name that OpenSubtitles v3 sends (`subtitleFileName`), trimmed, and an empty name or a bare extension is none; the release the file was made for (`movieReleaseName`) is kept as a second name for matching only, never shown, unless it only repeats the first; and the player keeps whether the addon found the file by the video's hash (`m` is "h"), the frame rate it was timed for (`fpsMilli`), and its season and episode, of which a film has none (OpenSubtitles sends 0 for both). A value of the wrong shape is dropped, never thrown on, and a number sent as text is read. Files are capped at 5 MB, gunzipped where the engine can (Chromium 80 and later), and decoded after a byte-order mark, a strict UTF-8 check and the language's code page. The six most recent files stay parsed while the player is open, and are let go when it closes. A drift fix (getting ahead, falling behind) stretches a file timed for another frame rate onto the video's clock, kept for each file as its timing is (`createSubtitleDrifts` in the core, at most 300). As a file is read, the adverts OpenSubtitles and other sites add are taken out (`stripAdCues`). A line goes wherever it is when each part of it is an advert, a site's address, or the words a site puts before its address ("Do you want subtitles for any video?", "Official YIFY movies site:", only as a whole part), when it says "Downloaded from" a site, or when it is a credit with the site's address on the next line. A credit on its own ("Subtitles by …", "Synced and corrected by …") goes only among the first or last three lines left once the adverts are out, and only when a few words of names follow "by": at most five, not ending in a full stop, and not starting with an ordinary word such as "the", "my" or "hand". A line of dialogue that mentions subtitles or a website stays, and so does dialogue next to a site's address. What the lines say about the file is kept in memory for that video, never stored (`profileSubtitles`): how many lines, where the last one ends (one or two lines set more than ten minutes after all the rest are left out), where the last three end, how long the lines are on screen (a stretch counted once however many lines show in it), which script its letters are in, and how many lines are for the hard of hearing (sounds in brackets, a speaker's name in capitals, a music sign alone). `judgeSubtitleFit` then judges the file against the video's length: the player's own once known, else the title's running time (then only "too long" past 125% of it, and no drift); none on a live broadcast or a video under five minutes. A file whose letters are mostly in another script than its language's (under one in five, from 200 letters) is in another language. For Chinese, Japanese and Korean, each Han, kana or Hangul character counts as three letters, as a line in those scripts is about a third as long as the line it stands for in an alphabet; a Japanese file also needs kana to be at least 5% of its kana and Han together, and a Chinese file has at most 20%, so English lines beside them change nothing. A file holds signs only when the name the addon gives it says "forced", "signs and songs" or "signs & songs", or, against a length to judge by, when it has under 1.2 lines a minute and under 150 lines. A name that says signs and songs is trusted however many lines the file has: the karaoke of its songs, one syllable to a line, gives it as many lines as a full file. One that only says "forced" is trusted too, except against a length to judge by when the file has at least 5 lines a minute, as a full file does ("Full+Forced" is a full file with the forced lines in it); lines drawn over another are not counted for this either, so the karaoke of a forced file's songs does not make it look full. "Non-forced", "non forced", "no forced" and "not forced" are no forced tag at all, in a file's name or a stream track's. "Forced" as the first word of a name with more words after it is the film's title (Forced.Vengeance.1982…) and does not count; the word alone or before the file's extension, or a tag later in the name, does. A file in another language or of signs only is unfit, and passed over as a file that cannot be read is. One that ends before 70% of the video and more than ten minutes early covers only part of it. So does one with under half the lines of the other files in its language that fit by their own reading whose lines are also on screen for under half as long as theirs (their medians), such as a forced file whose name does not say so. Lines drawn over another are not counted (`apart`: a line counts when it starts once every line before it has ended), so the karaoke of the songs, one syllable to a line drawn over the song's line, neither makes a plain file look like part of one beside a full file that has it, nor makes a signs and songs file whose name does not say so (most names say nothing of it) look like a whole one beside the others; lines and time both, so that a file that keeps each line on until the next starts (one made from a format with no end times), or a line left on for an hour by mistake, make an ordinary file beside it look so by its time; this needs a length to judge by, the title's running time being enough, and clears any drift on the file (`judgeSubtitleFits`). One whose lines run on past the video's end is falling behind (timed for film speed, 23.976 frames a second, on a 25 fps video) only on strong evidence: its last line ends at least 2.1% past the end (half of the 4.3% such a file runs on) and at least 60 s past it, and no more than 4.3% past it (plus 10 s, or 0.3% of the video when that is more), and its last three lines all end past it by more than that margin. Less than that, or one or two late lines (a translator's credit, a teaser for the next episode, a recap the video does not have, another release's ending), is taken as it is, with no drift; one that runs on further is too long. When at least two other files that fit the video as they are (no drift, and a last line no more than 15 minutes before the video's end and not past it) agree where the last line goes (more than half of them within 1% of their middle), they decide for a file that fits by its own reading and whose last three lines run on past the end: it falls behind when it ends about 4.3% after them (within 0.5%), even under 60 s past the end, and is too long (another cut, or a longer ending) when it does not (`judgeSubtitleFits`; only with the video's own length). No file is taken for getting ahead by itself. A file timed for the faster video it plays on ends about 4% before files that hide their drift in the end titles, and so does a plain file next to files that caption the song over the end titles; nothing tells the video's frame rate, so getting ahead is the viewer's to choose under Timing. A drift found this way is fixed by itself on the file showing, and Timing and the file's row say so; never on a file the viewer gave a drift of their own, nor on one with a timing an older build kept with no drift choice beside it, and "No fix" on such a file is kept for it (`'none'`), so it is not fixed again. Moving the timing of a file with no drift choice keeps `'auto'` for it with the timing (`driftKeptWithTiming`; never for the stream's own track, whose length shows no drift), where the player's fix is on or there is none to make on the stream playing: the fix stays on as the viewer sees it, and is worked out again from each stream's own evidence, so on another release of the video, which the file may fit as it is, its lines are not stretched, and on one whose length shows a drift the player still fixes it. A drift fix the viewer picks replaces it; pressing the one already in use changes nothing, so the player's own fix is never made a fixed drift for the file on every release. A file right for the video but a few seconds out, or timed for another frame rate, is lined up by itself against a reference known to fit the video (`alignSubtitles` in the core, `player/subtitle-align.ts`), from the timing of the lines alone, so the reference may be in another language. The aligner makes stretches of speech of each file's lines (lines drawn over another count once, gaps under 0.3 s are closed, a line counts 10 s at most) and, for the file as it is and for each change of frame rate (25 or 24 against 23.976 and back, 25 against 24 and back, 30 against 29.97 and back), lets every pair of stretches within 5 minutes of each other vote, in 50 ms steps, for the offsets that put their starts and their ends on each other: the correlation of the two files' speech edges. The highest peak is the answer, and its prominence is its height over the best answer that is clearly different (one moving the lines more than 5 s elsewhere on average). Then the film is cut into windows of at least two minutes and 40 stretches, ten at most, each lined up on its own near the answer. It is sure when the prominence is at least 2, at least 60% of the windows agree with the answer (within 0.5 s) with at most one off, a line fitted through their offsets moves under 0.6 s over the film (no drift left), no run of them jumps away from the rest (a different cut, an advert break), at least 60% of the file's lines land on the reference's speech, and both files have 40 lines and 5 minutes in common; it is none, nothing lining up, under a prominence of 1.6 (or 1.25 with three windows and 30% of them agreeing), and unsure otherwise, which moves nothing: so is a file whose two parts each line up a different way, and one against a reference still arriving that covers too little of it to say. Calibrated on OpenSubtitles files of 18 titles (10 films, 8 episodes), timings only, kept as start and end numbers in the core's fixtures with no text: of 308 pairs of files for the same video, 221 came out sure, 57 unsure and 30 none (19 of those with a prominence under 1.15, nothing to go on); of the sure, 209 agreed with an independent FFT study within 0.2 s at both ends of the film and 220 within 0.5 s, the worst by 0.79 s; of 22 pairs where one file was cut differently, 14 were unsure, 7 none and 1 sure; and all 449 pairs for another video (292 another film or another show, 157 another episode of the same show) came out none, the highest prominence 1.40. A pair takes about 8 ms on a desktop (28 ms at most; a three-hour film's 3,719 lines against itself 13 to 15 ms, against another file's 2,836 lines 20 to 25 ms), and 0.2 to 0.4 s for those with the JIT turned off, so on the TV it runs in slices of 8 ms with the page given its turn between them, one file at a time, in memory only. The reference, best first: the stream's own subtitle track in any language, once its lines reach 75% of the video's length (its lines come in step with the picture); a file read as whole with no drift and linked to the video by its hash (`linkedToVideo`); one read so and named for its release with a strong match (the same name, or the group confirming it); then the stream's track while its lines still arrive. A group of files that agree among themselves is never a reference, as they may all be for another cut, and with no reference nothing is moved. A reference needs 40 lines. Once found it is kept while it still is one and nothing of a strictly better kind has come, so files are not lined up against one and then another. A track can be a reference only where the engine hands its lines over without showing them, so it is selected, unseen, while a file shows: hls.js and Shaka always, Android only for a track it says is text (the codecs of a track Media3 reads into its own cues say what it was: PGS, VobSub and DVB are pictures, which Android draws itself; ADR 0005), never one of forced lines only, and never on the TV's own player, which lists no tracks. The session keeps the timing of every line a track hands over (`streamTimings`), so Android's lines count after it lets them go. Against a track still arriving, files are lined up at 20 and 40 minutes of its lines and once it is complete, three runs at most and none once sure, and it says a file is for another video for good only when it has no stretch of five minutes without a line (one the viewer skipped through has gaps the aligner would take for silence). Each file the planner reads is lined up, in any language. A sure answer moves the file's lines, offset and rate, decided once for each file in a stream: before the file's first line shows (within the hold, item 10), it is made; after, only when it moves the lines at least 0.3 s, at most twice a stream, as lines that jump about are worse than lines a little out (`decideMove`). The viewer's choices come first: a version picked by hand still shows, moved where it can be; a drift fix the viewer chose for the file means no move at all, and so does a timing they set on this stream before the move came; a timing they set on lines the player moved is kept with the drift choice `'aligned'` and adds to the move, on this release and wherever the file is lined up again. A timing kept with no such mark was set on the file's own lines, on another release or before the player lined it up, and is set aside where the file is moved rather than added to it, so a kept timing and a move never stack: the move already puts the lines where this video has them; on a stream where the file is not moved, that timing is used as before (`timingInUse`, `driftKeptWithTiming`). The move's rate stands for the drift fix while the viewer chose none, and shows as such on the drift row.
10. **Choosing subtitles.** A sheet (Blue, or the player's Audio & subtitles chip) rises from the bottom with four tabs that say what is on now: Subtitles, Audio, Timing and Style. The picture above it stays bright and the subtitle line sits just above it. Subtitles lists Off and the languages, the last chosen first, and beside them the language in focus's versions: the stream's own tracks first (always in step), then each addon's files, named by source and numbered where a source sent several, with the name the addon gives the file, a subtitle extension left off (OpenSubtitles v3 sends each file's own name, and the release it was made for, which is only matched). Rows say "Loading…" until the first line, "Couldn't load" when a file can't be read (it gives way to the next in its language, which then says "skipped"), "Linked to your video" when the addon found the file by the video's hash, someone having linked that file to this very video file, and its names do not say another release (`linkedToVideo`), and otherwise "Same release" when the file's names and the stream's file name or title name the same release with a strong match (`fileReleaseMatch`, a guess). Before a name is read, the tags a subtitle file puts after its release are taken off its end, one at a time and a dozen at most (`readNameTags`): its extension; its language, as an ISO 639 code, a site's own code or the language's name ("…-NTb.en.srt", "….English.srt", "…-NTb13_[spa].srt" with a pack's number before it); that it is for the hard of hearing ("-HI", "(SDH)", "[cc]") or is not ("-NHI", ".NonHI", ".Non-HI"); which disc it goes with ("-CD1"); a second version ("v2"); and the site it came from ("….C.updated.Addic7ed.com"), so that none of them is read as the release's group, as "HI" was in "…-CAKES-HI.srt"; a name of one word keeps it ("It.srt" is the film). A file's facts come from its own name first; the second name OpenSubtitles sends fills only what that leaves out, so a fact the file's own name gives against the stream ("…1080p…-NTb.ita.srt" on NTb's 720p stream) is never outweighed by a second name that leaves it out ("AMZN.WEB-DL.DDP5.1.H.264-NTb"), and a name that gives two resolutions ("WEBRip.720p/1080p") gives neither. Two names match when, read so, they are the same name and it gives the group or two facts ("Inception" says nothing of the release, and "Dune.Part.Two.2024.4K" only its resolution), or when they agree on at least two of episode, resolution, family of source and group, and differ on none, so the same group's 720p and 1080p still do not match. The families of source are the web, Blu-ray, HDTV and DVD, and each kind of copy made in the cinema on its own (camcorder, telesync, telecine and screener, HDCAM and HDTS with them), as each is timed its own way; a service's tag or WEB-DL is read before a cinema word, and "web" alone last, so "The.TS.Madison…WEB-DL" comes from the web. YTS and YIFY, which tag their releases ("[YTS.MX]", ".YIFY") rather than end them on a group, count as one group. A match is strong when it is the same name, or both names give a group and the groups agree; with a group on one side only, or none, it is loose, as another service's or another group's file of the same episode, resolution and source says as much. Over Torrentio's lists for three titles (1,388 streams, against OpenSubtitles v3's English files), strong matches were right in all 38 pairs that could be judged and wrong in none; loose ones were right in 1 and wrong in 8, mostly streams whose names end on a group that cannot be read (" - AnoZu"), so a loose match says nothing on its row and never jumps the queue. Rows keep their places while more addons answer (`keepOrder`). Each stream starts in the last chosen language. First comes the version picked by hand for this release, when it is offered and can be read: even before the stream's own track, whether or not the stream has listed its tracks yet (hls.js, Shaka and Android list them before the first picture), and taking over from an automatic choice when its addon answers later. Else the stream's own track, unless it holds forced lines only (marked by the stream, `FORCED=YES` in HLS or Shaka's or Android's own flag, or named "Forced" or "Signs & Songs", not "Non-Forced": `isForcedName`). Else the addon file that fits best (`rankSubtitles`): one made for the release (`madeForRelease`: named for it with a strong match, or found by the video's hash with no name saying another release), while it is not read yet or reads whole with no drift (once read as drifting, covering only part, too long or unfit, it ranks by its fit like any other), and so does a file lined up against the video for sure (item 9), its drift counted as fixed by the move, so a plain file lined up comes before a reference for the hard of hearing and before a plain one only by the rule for plain files below; then the best fit (whole and plain; then for the hard of hearing; then one with a drift to fix; then one whose timing matches nothing in the video's once lined up, kept rather than passed over; then one covering only part of the video or too long), and among files that fit as well, one found by the video's hash and named for the release, then one named for it, then one found by the hash alone, then one loosely named for it, then the addon's order. OpenSubtitles marks every file linked to the hash, and some are other releases' (on LOL's HDTV file of Fringe S04E03, REWARD's DVDRip timed at 25 frames a second among them), so a hash match whose names give a fact against the stream (episode, resolution, family of source or group) is not made for the release and counts as a loose match at most. A file whose own name or whose addon's season and episode say it is for another episode or season than the one playing is unfit whatever reading finds: its second release name counts only when neither says anything, as OpenSubtitles' is often cut short ("Fallout.2024.S01E0"), a whole season's ("S01E00-10") or another season's by mistake, and episode 0 alone is no episode. An IMDb or TMDB episode's video id says which is playing ("tt0944947:1:2", "tmdb:1399:1:2"); a Kitsu or MyAnimeList id is the anime's number and the episode's, with no season, so there, as for a film's id, the stream's names say. Such a file is passed over even when nothing else is left, unless picked by hand; its row says "For another episode", dimmed, before it is read. A file named as one disc of a release split in two or more ("CD1", "cd.2", "Disc 1", "Part 1 of 2", or "part1" after a group) covers only part of the video until it has been read against the video's length, so it never goes before a whole file, unless the stream's own name is that disc. Plain files come before ones for the hard of hearing unless the viewer picked one of those by hand for this title: by what reading found, and among files not read yet by their names ("HI", "SDH" or "CC" after another word, or "hearing impaired", but not the "Hi" of "Hi.Score.Girl", and never a name that says "NonHI", "Non-HI" or "NHI", whatever else it says: LGND names its plain files "….NonHI.cc.en.LGND"). When every file in the language could not be read or is unfit, one in the language's script that can be read shows (signs only is better than nothing), then the stream's track of forced lines only, then a file in another script, then one that could not be read; the track of forced lines only gives way to a full track, or to a file that can be used, when one comes, and an automatic file that then could not be read, or reads as another script, gives way to it again. The version picked by hand, or a file made for the release while that counts, shows at once, with no hold (a loose match on the names is read and held like any other file), and the other files made for the release (two addons' copies, or a plain copy beside one for the hard of hearing) are read only to see whether one fits clearly better: those placed above it, or all of them once it has read as less than whole and plain (or as wanted for the hard of hearing), or while a file made for the release is still downloading 2 s after its load started, when the first of them read shows in its place, not counted as a switch, as none of its lines had shown. When the version picked by hand reads as running on past the video's end line after line (its last line and the ends of its last three past the end by more than releases differ by, as above) with no drift its own lines show, the first two in the list are read beside it for their checks only, so a drift that only other files agreeing on an end can show is fixed again each time it plays; it stays whatever they show. Otherwise files are read in the list's order (the stream's own files first, then each addon's as it sent them), two at a time: the first two always, more only while none of those read fits as it is, and at most four in each language for each stream (`MAX_EXTRA_CHECKS` beyond the first), so another stream of the video (a failover, Quality or Sources) reads its own first two again. The first line waits until those being read have been, and lined up where there is a reference, and the player knows the video's length, for at most 2 s after the first file was read, and then the best read so far shows (`planSubtitlePick`). A file still downloading 2 s after it was asked for is not waited for and no longer counts among the two, so the next in the list is read meanwhile. When the hold ends with none read, the first placed is chosen; a choice whose lines have not shown yet gives way at once to the best file read, and that is not counted as a switch, as nothing changes on screen. After that a file read later takes over only when it fits clearly better (a whole step: no drift in place of a drift, plain lines in place of ones for the hard of hearing, the whole film in place of part of it), at most twice and within 90 s of the first line, and more files are read only while a switch could follow; a file made for the release, from an addon that answers late, takes over too. Two made for the release (two addons' copies, or CD1 and CD2) are not swapped back and forth as each is read: the second takes over from the first only as any file read later does. Nothing takes over from the viewer's own choice. Moving the timing or choosing a drift fix of the automatic file showing makes it the viewer's own: nothing takes over from it, and its lines show at once. A language turned on from the sheet is the viewer's for the title, whatever the audio heard, and the version in it is chosen as at the start of a stream, with the same reads, hold and switches: it starts on its best placed version (`languageFirstPick`: the version picked by hand for the release, then the stream's own track unless it holds forced lines only, then the files as `rankSubtitles` places them by what reading them has found so far, passing over unfit ones, then the list's order). Pressing the version already showing changes nothing, unless it could not be read, when it is tried again. A version picked from a language's list is kept for the release playing once it has been read (`createSubtitlePickMemory` in the core: the title, the release by its group, its binge group or the video, and the file by the same hash as its timing; never a link; at most 300, for each profile, on this screen only, ADR 0006); one given up on while it loaded is not kept, picking the stream's own track forgets it, and picking a language keeps nothing. Lab streams and BannerTV Demo's subtitle checks keep nothing, and there the version a language starts on stays as pressed. Rows also say, two notes at most, before the release name so that a long name cut at its end never hides why a row is dimmed, what reading a file found or its name says: "Looks like another language", "Only a few lines" and "For another episode" (all dimmed, still there to pick), "Covers only part of the video", "Longer than this video", then "Synced to your video" (lined up against the video, or the file others are lined up against, which says its drift, link and release too) or else "Drift fixed for you", then "Linked to your video" or "Same release", and "For the hard of hearing". Timing says what lining the file showing up did: "Lined up by BannerTV: +2.3 s" (and stretched to the video's frame rate, where it was), already in step, the file the others are checked against, still being checked, couldn't be lined up, left as the viewer set it, checked too late in the film to move, or "Couldn't check this file against your video" with no reference to check it against; its tab says "lined up" beside the timing when lines were moved. The phone's panel says the same. Settings > Diagnostics has a Subtitles section on the newest play (beside a TV's own rows and the Lab's speeds its column has room for one), and its line in the console carries the last three, newest first, kept in memory only and never stored (`player/subtitle-trace.ts`): the video's id; whether the hash of the file playing was read and in how long, sent by the stream's addon, failed and why (the server ignored the byte range, hid the file's size, the file is too small, the request was refused, or it took over 4 s) or was not tried and why (a playlist, a link needing request headers, or no file link at all, as for a torrent); how many files came in each language; why the version showing was chosen (picked by hand, the stream's own track, linked to the video by its hash, the same release name with the group confirming it or with no group, synced to the video, the best fit of the files read, the list's order, or off) and whether the viewer turned its language on; what the files were lined up against (the stream's track, a file linked by hash, a file named for the release, or nothing) and, for each file lined up, its language, how sure, the offset and rate, and the milliseconds it took; and how long the first line took from the player opening. It says ids, counts, language codes and times only, never a link, an addon's address or a file's name; an addon's video id that is itself a link is kept only as being one. Where the tuning turns performance marks on, the subtitles being asked for, the hash's outcome and the first line are marked too (`sub:asked`, `sub:hash:*`, `sub:firstLine`), and each file lined up with how sure (`sub:aligned:*`), beside `sub:chosen:*`. Timing moves in 0.1 s and 0.5 s steps and is kept for each subtitle file under a hash of the video and the file (`createSubtitleOffsets`, at most 300; never a link). Size, colour, background and position are chosen from samples, with a sample line while nobody speaks; Settings shows the same choices in the same sheet. Back goes from a tab's choices to the tabs, then closes onto the controls; the pointer passing over a tab or a language does not switch it, a click does.
11. **Audio.** The TV's `audioTracks` (direct play) or hls.js and Shaka's own lists, with channels and format where hls.js and Shaka give them (`CHANNELS` and `CODECS` in HLS, Shaka's channel count, codecs, spatial audio and DASH roles) and the commentary mark (an HTML track kind, a DASH role, or "commentary" in the name). The TV's own player gives only a language and the name inside the file. Shaka's tracks are known by what they are (the codec by family, as Shaka groups them: it lists HE-AAC and AAC-LC as one track named after whichever plays), not by their place in its list, which changes with the picture quality. hls.js gives each rendition its group's whole codec list, so a rendition in a group whose codecs are of different formats shows no format (Atmos still shows, from its channels; HE-AAC and AAC-LC together are still AAC). The language picked is kept for the title (and becomes the profile's own only with "Use player choices for every title", ADR 0004 item 7), and the next stream starts in it, on a track that is not a commentary when the language has another (on sport only the stream's own mark counts, as its main sound is often named commentary); a stream handed from one engine to another is matched again.
12. **Resume and progress.** The position is saved every 10 s, on pause, when the app is hidden and on leaving. A title resumes once 30 s have played, but not in the last minute; a rewatch resumes unless it stopped past 90%. The clock is only trusted once it has moved past the resume point, never from the 0 that webOS reports first. Details offers "Resume from 42:10" and "Start over", and a series its latest episode. Episodes show a progress bar or a watched tick. A live broadcast, which only the engine reports (no length from the TV's player for anything but a Matroska or WebM file, a playlist with no end in hls.js, a dynamic manifest in Shaka), saves no progress and no Continue Watching card, has no Up Next, has Start over dimmed, and leaves the player when it ends. Every other stream of it (failover, step-down, Quality, Sources, Try again) starts at the live edge, since its clock means nothing on another source's. A broadcast an older build saved with a place to resume has that entry cleared and is opened again at the live edge. Nothing is saved while the length is unknown (before the stream's metadata, or once a failed stream is let go of), and an entry saved with no known length is never resumed. A film whose file gives no length is saved against the title's running time.
13. **Up Next.** In the last 30 seconds a card offers the next aired episode, counting down to the real end of this one (`secondsToEnd`, at the speed playing), so nothing is cut; the next starts as this one ends. It starts the same release (`bingeGroup`) from the same addon, else the automatic choice. Its streams are asked for two minutes before the end, so reading a long list does not hold up the card, and every count runs on the clock, not on timer ticks, which a busy TV runs late. The card shows only over the picture alone and takes focus only then: the controls, a sheet, a skip, the problem box and the sleep screen all win, and with the controls up the Next episode button counts instead ("Next in 12 s"). When it goes, the player puts focus back on Pause or the picture. Up or Down on it brings the controls up in its place, as over the picture (so does either on the sleep timer's warning); Left and Right move between its buttons. Back or Keep watching dismisses it; a rewind out of the last stretch and back in brings it back (`rearms`). An end reached while something held the count (`endAction`) counts 10 s once it goes; an end the viewer saw counting plays on. Before an episode that would start by itself, "Still watching?" asks first when two have already started by themselves since the last key or pointer move, or after 90 minutes with nothing pressed (`askStillWatching`; Settings can turn it off); Stop or Back goes back to the series.
14. **Remote controls.** OK plays or pauses and shows the controls; with a skip on its way it lands the skip instead, and while a stream is still starting it waits for the picture and then shows the controls. One skip rule for every way of skipping: Left and Right, and Fast forward and Rewind, each add 10 s a press, growing to 30 s and 60 s while a key is held, and the total lands once, 0.8 s after the last press. With the controls hidden a small note shows the total and where it lands. A live broadcast has no bar to skip along: Fast forward and Rewind jump 10 s at once within what it keeps and show the controls. Where the engine can say where live is (`liveWindow`: hls.js's sync position, Shaka's seek range, the TV's own player its seekable range less 10 s), every jump on a broadcast is kept between the oldest moment kept and live (`clampToWindow`), the controls show how far behind live it is (`judgeLive`, with a segment's slack and half that to count as live again, so the reading does not flicker; judged every second, so a paused broadcast falls behind too), and Go to live jumps back. Up and Down show the controls, and so does a notch of the Magic Remote wheel, which never skips (Eyitayo's call on 30 October 2026, during the step 2 TV check); with the controls up the wheel moves a row a notch, as it does everywhere, and stops at the bar as Up does; with focus still on the picture, as the pointer leaves it when it brings the controls up, a notch goes to Pause. The rest of the roll that brought the controls up is spent until the wheel rests for half a second, however long it goes on: on the TV a quick roll comes in flicks a quarter of a second apart, and the combined TV check of 31 October saw one run on from Pause to Audio & subtitles when only gaps under 150 ms counted. Once the controls hide, the next notch brings them up again. The controls hide after 5 s of playing, but not while a panel or the sheet is open (they make way for the sheet and come back when it goes), while the pointer rests on the title or the bar, or while Start over offers to go back. The controls are two tiers in fixed places (the plan's Player controls table): the transport (Start over, Back 10 s, Play or Pause, Forward 10 s, and Next episode on anything with an episode list, or Go to live on a broadcast) as icons with their names under them, and the tools (Audio & subtitles, Quality, Sources, Episodes, Info) as chips saying what is on now, which Audio Guidance reads with their names. Up and Down land on Pause and on the first tool whatever had focus before, and Left and Right stay in a row. What can't be used now stays, dimmed, with a short reason read with its name (under an icon while it is focused, in place of a chip's value all the time), so nothing moves under the remote: Start over on a broadcast ("No start on live"), Next episode with no next one ("Last episode", "Not out yet", or "No episode list" until the list arrives), Quality with one quality found ("Only 1080p found"), Episodes with no list, and Forward 10 s and Go to live while a broadcast plays live ("Playing live"; Go to live says "Live point unknown" where the engine can't tell). Back 10 s and Forward 10 s follow the skip rule; on a broadcast they jump at once, as Rewind and Fast forward do. Start over, everywhere but a broadcast, offers "Back to" the place it left for 8 s afterwards (from 10 s in). The controls win over Up Next and the sleep timer's warning: each card shows only while the controls are hidden. Play, Pause and Stop work everywhere; behind the "won't play" box only Stop does. Blue opens and closes Audio & subtitles. Back cancels a skip, hides the controls, then leaves; just after the controls hid by themselves it says "Press Back again to leave." first. The pointer: a click on the picture shows or hides the controls, a click on the bar jumps there, and a back arrow at the top left leaves. On the TV the Magic Remote's OK is a click alone while the pointer shows (the step 2 TV check saw no key press with any click), so after the wheel moved focus a click on the picture presses the focused control, as OK would. Sources, Episodes and Info open sheets over the picture, which keeps playing, as Audio & subtitles does (item 18). The Magic Remote OK can come as a key press as well as the click, so a click within 400 ms of an OK that something answered is the same press and presses nothing more, not even a control the key press just brought up under the pointer; an OK that nothing answered, such as one a text box took, leaves the click to press what the pointer is on. A layer the key press opened is not closed by its click landing outside it. With the pointer on the picture the key press is the picture's too (or lands a skip on its way), not the press of whichever control kept focus, unless the wheel has moved focus, or brought the controls up on Pause, since the pointer last moved. Arrows, the wheel and the pointer do nothing while a stream starts. Quality on a paused film opens the other stream paused, and failover keeps the viewer's pause.
15. **The picture for the LG store.** The video element sits in the page body, outside the app root that `main.tsx` scales on small windows, at 0,0 and the window's full size with no transform: LG's store rule, without which webOS starts its screensaver over a playing video.
16. **Keys stay private.** Stream links and addon addresses can carry debrid or API keys, so they never appear in focus keys, error messages, logs or stored data; streams are keyed by the addon's place in the list, its id and the stream's position, so two installs of one addon stay apart. A subtitle file sent with no id of its own (its link stands in for one) is keyed by a hash of that link.
17. **Continue Watching.** Home's first row is built from the saved progress and a small card per video the player has played (name, artwork, episode, and the episode after it with its release time), at most 200 cards, never a stream link. A title is on the row when it resumes, when an episode was barely started (from the start; a barely started film is left out), or when it was finished or stopped in its last minute and the next episode has aired, at most 20 titles, most recent first. OK goes straight into the player. Progress saved before the cards existed gets its card from the meta addon once a session, while Home is on show. The row is worked out again only while Home is on show, and on the hour for an episode that has just aired. Back from the player lands on the title played, wherever the row now has it. No link test or extra artwork size runs for the row.
18. **Sheets, the sleep timer and speed.** Sources lists every stream the addons offered in All streams' order and filter, with the one playing ticked and always listed; it asks the addons nothing and tests nothing, as the decoder is the film's. Episodes shows seasons on the left and the season in focus's episodes on the right, with no images. Info shows the stream (addon, release, size, picture, sound, how it is played) and the title or episode; OK or Back closes it. Its other tabs, in place of the plan's "More" chip so the tools row keeps five chips, are Speed and Sleep timer. The sleep timer offers 15 to 90 minutes or the end of the episode or film (not on a broadcast), lasts into the next episode of the same title, and a minute before it pauses shows a corner card (Add 15 min, Turn off) over the picture alone; then the video pauses under a dark screen. Its "Carry on watching" plays on from there (the combined TV check of 31 October found a second OK needed when it only woke the screen); Back wakes it still paused. "End of episode" pauses at the end instead of the next one starting. Speed shows only where Settings > Lab measured this TV keeping that speed, with sound, for the kind of stream playing (`speedKindOf`: the TV's player on a file or on HLS, hls.js, Shaka; never DASH on the TV's player): in the Lab, Info's Speed tab measures normal speed as the yardstick, then 0.75×, 1.25× and 1.5× (`clockRate`, a least-squares slope over 2.4 s of readings, within 8%, and the TV not putting the speed back), asks the tester whether there is sound at each that kept time, and checks normal speed comes back (`judgeClock`, `judgeSpeed`). `createSpeedMemory` keeps, per kind and for the whole TV, the speeds that passed, why one failed, when, and the firmware version; never a link. Diagnostics lists them. Every stream starts at normal speed, and "Ends at" and Up Next's count follow the speed playing. A broadcast offers no speed.

## Consequences

- Most viewers press Watch and get a picture. When the guess is wrong, the next stream starts by itself, and All streams is one press away.
- On a fast connection a 4K web release wins over a 4K remux until a remux the viewer picks plays ten minutes without buffering; after that the limit has risen and remuxes rank by quality again.
- Asking addons on a focus rest costs requests the viewer may never use. The rest delay, the five-minute cache and the never-on-Home rule keep that small; the link test is the costly part and is the first thing to turn off if the floor TV struggles.
- Embedded subtitles in direct-play files are still missing. The Lab tests the webOS media pipeline's Luna calls (`subscribe` for the track list, `setSubtitleEnable` and `selectTrack` by `mediaId`), which Lampa, Stremio and Nuvio use. LG does not document them, and on webOS 25 selecting a text track has frozen HLS playback. On the webOS 22 TV they answer OK and list the tracks but drew no subtitle in two runs (see below), so the player does not use them and the subtitle menu offers addon subtitles.
- ASS styling, positioning and karaoke are Phase 5 (JASSUB on webOS 6+). Torrents and proxy-header streams wait for the Phase 5 streaming server.
- The LG demo addon's adaptive HLS needs nothing special: the TV's player takes it, and hls.js takes over if it has subtitle or audio renditions.
