# Sports rights: what lower-league football costs

What it would cost BannerTV to stream, or partner with, the English National League and lower leagues in other countries. Researched on 30 October 2026 from league, club and broadcaster announcements, accounts, rights tenders and trade press. A second, independent pass re-checked the key figures against their sources and corrected or dropped what did not hold up. Main sources are listed at the end.

Most lower-league deals are never published. Where a value is undisclosed this page says so, and any estimate is marked as an estimate. Money is per season and in pounds unless stated, at about €1 = £0.83 to £0.86, $1 = £0.75 and the rates shown in each section. Recheck rates before relying on a figure.

## The short answer

- **The English National League cannot be bought until 2031.** DAZN holds exclusive UK and worldwide live rights to the National League, National League North and National League South for seven seasons, from 2024-25 to the end of 2030-31. The fee is undisclosed; the league calls the deal a joint venture. Before DAZN the National League was cheap: BT Sport's first deal (2013) was reported at £300,000, and broadcasters paid the two clubs £5,000 to £8,000 per televised match.
- **Below the National League it costs very little.** Steps 3 and 4 sell streaming at about £1,000 a match, which ClubberTV pays under a three-year deal from 2025-26. Steps 5 and 6 have no central deals: clubs can agree streams themselves with their league's consent, often for no fee, and the cost is production (£30 to £3,500 a match).
- **Leagues in the rest of the UK and Ireland now run their own streaming services** (LOITV, NIFL TV, Cymru Football TV, SPFL Player). They want distribution, not a buyer. The realistic deal is £0 in rights fees plus a revenue share or referral fee.
- **UK rights to small foreign leagues range from free to low six figures a season.** Many are unsold or streamed free on YouTube in the UK. When money changes hands it ranges from nothing plus a revenue share, through tens of thousands, to about £375,000 for a star-driven league across several countries.
- **Every live stream in the UK must respect the Saturday 2:45pm to 5:15pm blackout**, at every level and for foreign leagues too. It may end from 2029-30.

**What earlier holders paid.** Premier Sports (2010-13) paid £5,000 to the home club and £1,000 to the away club per live match. BT Sport's first deal (2013) was reported at £300,000, with 25 to 30 live games in its first season. In 2015 BT paid £6,000 home and £1,000 away; by 2020-21 the away fee was £2,000. BT's 2018 renewal and its December 2020 extension to 2023-24 (inherited by TNT Sports) were never valued publicly.

**The league's own service, before DAZN.** From December 2022 to November 2024 the league ran NLTV itself, built by StreamAMG. A UK matchday pass was £9.50 and an international season pass £170. In its first half-season it took £1.4m gross from 117,000 subscribers in 57 countries and paid £750,000 back to clubs. Net revenue was split 60% to the club the subscriber picked, 25% shared centrally and 15% to the league. One fan blog says DAZN kept that split; no primary source confirms it.

**How the clubs see it.** In September 2026 Boreham Wood's owner said National League football was going "for no real money" and that clubs "get literally a pittance, or nothing".

**What is open to BannerTV.**

1. Linking to DAZN's NLTV. No rights fee is needed to send people to the official app. A referral or revenue share needs a contract with DAZN.
2. A sublicence from DAZN. No terms are public and there is no sign DAZN sublicenses to consumer apps. The nearest precedent is Sky sublicensing EFL games to ITV, reportedly for £15m.
3. Free official clips that DAZN, the league and clubs post on YouTube and TikTok, embedded under each platform's terms. That is not a licence.
4. The next tender, for 2031-32 onwards. On old prices, a 30-match package would cost roughly £0.23m to £0.69m a season including production (30 × (£6,000 to £8,000 club fees + £1,800 to £15,000 production)). This is a rough estimate.

## Rules every live stream in the UK must follow

- **The 3pm blackout (UEFA Article 48).** No live football may be shown in the UK on Saturdays from 2:45pm to 5:15pm. This applies at every level and to foreign leagues. In 2026-27 it applies every Saturday from 22 August 2026 to 24 April 2027, except 26 September, 3 October, 14 November and 26 December 2026 and 27 March 2027. Clubs at steps 1 to 6 may stream during those hours only if the stream is geo-blocked out of the UK and Crown Dependencies.
- **The blackout may go.** In August 2026 Premier League chief executive Richard Masters said a decision is due in about 18 months, ahead of the next tenders. The SPFL's October 2026 tender lets bidders take every match from 2029-30, Saturday 3pm included. The Cymru Premier streams every match. The FA, Scottish FA and Irish FA all have to agree, because rights are sold UK-wide.
- **For BannerTV, any live football needs reliable geo-blocking and time-of-day blocking.**

## Other costs

**Production, per match.** From a vendor table (Pixellot, about 2020, converted at $1 = £0.75) and UK freelance price lists:

| Set-up | Cost per match |
| --- | --- |
| Outside-broadcast truck, 6 to 12 cameras | £7,500 to £15,000 |
| Remote production, 4 to 6 cameras | £3,000 to £4,500 |
| Automation plus two manned cameras | £675 to £940 |
| One fixed automated camera | £30 to £75 |
| UK freelance crew, one camera | £200 to £500 |
| UK freelance crew, two to four cameras with commentary | £1,200 to £3,500 |

A Veo Cam 3 costs £1,199, plus £42 to £84 a month and £10 a month for live streaming: about £1,800 to £2,600 per camera in the first year. Albion Rovers' fixed Pixellot install was paid for with an £18,000 donation in 2020.

**Delivery and protection.** DRM services that work on LG TVs start at about £150 to £225 a month (EZDRM, castlabs, Axinom), plus hosting and delivery. webOS supports PlayReady and Widevine through standard HTML5 media extensions, with hardware-level Widevine from webOS 3.5. Small leagues usually ask for DRM and geo-blocking. Big ones ask for much more: LaLiga's 2026 international tender requires anti-VPN checks, forensic watermarking and checks on where subscribers are. Consumer licences normally exclude betting use; official Premier League, EFL and SPFL data belongs to Genius Sports until 2029.

**Ofcom.** BannerTV today only plays addons that users install, so it may fall outside Ofcom's rules. If it curated a library of licensed programmes, that would probably be an on-demand programme service (ODPS). It must be notified to Ofcom before launch, with no fee below £10m turnover in 2026/27; Ofcom is consulting on new fees for 2027/28. Live streams are not an ODPS. A channel reachable through a regulated programme guide needs a TV licence: £2,500 to apply and at least £1,000 a year. New guide rules came into force in June 2026, with licences needed from 1 December 2027.

## What this means for BannerTV

1. **Budget nothing for National League rights before 2031.** The most that is possible is sending viewers to DAZN's NLTV, ideally with a referral deal.
2. **Start with free official streams.** WSL 2, USL, NWSL+, MLS Next Pro, the ISL, the Lowland League and selected NPFL games are free and official in the UK today. They cost nothing in rights; they need the platform's embed terms and, ideally, the rights holder's written consent.
3. **Then go to the league-owned services that want reach.** Cymru Football TV and NIFL TV have no LG TV app that we found, so BannerTV can offer to be one. LOITV has an LG app and unsold UK rights, so a referral or co-marketing deal fits. SPFL Player looks contractually barred from TV apps until 2029. Expected cost: £0 in rights plus a revenue share. Still to check: whether a webOS app can open another app (LOITV, DAZN) at a given match.
4. **If BannerTV wants rights of its own,** the affordable ones are steps 3 to 6, FA Trophy and FA Vase early rounds, and FA Cup qualifying picks. Budget about £1,000 a match in rights plus £200 to £3,500 in production. Every stream needs blackout handling and geo-blocking.
5. **UK rights to a foreign league** range from free with a revenue share to tens of thousands of pounds a season. Windows worth watching: Serie B (2027-28, with Destination Calcio as the precedent), Poland's I liga (tender open now), and the NPFL, Ghana Premier League and League of Ireland, where UK rights look unsold and there are diaspora audiences.
6. **Expect every partner to ask about piracy.** The League of Ireland says about half its audience watches illegally, costing it about €1m a year, and BannerTV plays content from third-party addons. Have a clear answer before the first meeting, for example a store build that carries only official sport. Sports currently ships in the sideload build only (`apps/webos/src/app/features.ts`). PLAN.md already asks for a legal review before payments or hosted services; a rights deal needs one too.

## Gaps

- Undisclosed: the DAZN National League fee, ClubberTV's total value and exclusivity, and the BBC, S4C, Virgin Media, Premier Sports and most foreign second-tier deals. Some figures are press estimates and are marked as such above.
- Some 2026 details rest on a single secondary source: SPFL Player's device limit (read on a development site), the Regionalliga Nord move to Leagues.football, DFB Play being free abroad, and the Eredivisie's 2025-30 UK partners.
- It was not confirmed whether LOITV applies the blackout in the UK, or whether National League North and South clubs may stream games DAZN does not pick.
- Prices and deals change often. Check anything here before relying on it.
